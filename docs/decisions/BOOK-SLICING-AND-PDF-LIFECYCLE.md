# How We Slice a Big Book, and the Full Life of a PDF

**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Author:** Dev 1
**For:** engineering management — written in plain language, no jargon.
**Status:** research complete, decision pending.

Every number in this document was **measured on this machine**, against a real
1,151-page textbook (*Dive into Deep Learning*, 44.7 MB), not estimated.

---

## 1. The short answer

The book does have a built-in chapter list. We just never look at it.

Every PDF can carry its own clickable chapter list — the thing that appears in
the sidebar of a PDF reader. Our code has **never once read it**. I checked the
whole API for the words `outline`, `bookmark`, and `toc`: there are no hits
except in unrelated comments.

I tested a real 1,151-page textbook:

| What we asked | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does it have a built-in chapter list? | **Yes — 1,335 entries** |
| How many are real top-level chapters? | **27** |
| How long does reading it take? | **4 seconds** |
| What does it cost? | **Nothing** |
| Do the page numbers actually point at the right pages? | **27 out of 27 correct** |
| Does it need a new library? | **No — already installed** |

The library is `pypdfium2`, which we already ship and already use. It is
Apache-2.0 licensed, so there is no licence problem. The function is `get_toc()`.
This is not a purchase, a new vendor, or a research project. It is roughly
forty lines of code we never wrote.

---

## 2. The real bottleneck is not what we assumed

We have been assuming that reading a big PDF is slow. **It is not.**

Measured on the 1,151-page book:

| Job | Per page | Whole book |
|---|---|---|
| Reading the text | 7 ms | **8 seconds** |
| Rendering pictures at our required quality | 61 ms | 1.2 minutes |
| **Looking for tables on every page** | **579 ms** | **11.1 minutes** |

Reading a 1,151-page book's text takes **8 seconds**. The slowness is almost
entirely one step: we run a table-detector over **every single page**, and it
costs 579 milliseconds each time. That one step is about 90% of the total.

This matters for the decision, because it means we do **not** need faster
hardware or a bigger time budget. We need to stop doing whole-book work in one
go. Once we process one chapter at a time (about 40 pages), that same step costs
**26 seconds** instead of 11 minutes — and it fits comfortably.

---

## 3. How to slice a book — the recommended method

A ladder of five checks. Stop at the first one that works.

| Rung | Method | Time on 1,000 pages | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Read the book's built-in chapter list.** Take only the top level. | 4 seconds | $0 |
| 2 | If absent: read the *printed* contents page and correct for the page-number offset (printed page 1 is often physical page 41). | under 1 second | $0 |
| 3 | If absent: use the font size, boldness and position we **already extract today and then throw away**. Ignore anything repeating in the same spot on many pages — that is a running header, not a chapter. | free, already computed | $0 |
| 4 | Score what is left against chapter word patterns (`Chapter 7:`, `Part II`, `Unit 3`). | under 1 second | $0 |
| 5 | Last resort before guessing: send the cheap model one line per page (page number, biggest font, first 200 characters) and ask which pages start a chapter. **Measured: 227,631 characters ≈ 57,000 tokens.** | 10–30 seconds | **$0.0085** |
| 6 | If all five fail: cut into fixed 30-page blocks, record `boundary_confidence = 'fallback'`, and surface it in the admin screen. | instant | $0 |

**Total: under one minute, under two cents, paid once per book — not per lesson.**

Rung 6 must never be silent. A lesson that begins mid-chapter has no beginning,
and a student will notice immediately. Under our own rule 5 in `CLAUDE.md`, a
documented limitation is not an accepted one — it needs a register ID.

### One caveat, stated honestly

**Every PDF in our repo has zero bookmarks.** All five test fixtures and the demo
chapter return nothing. That is *not* evidence that rung 1 fails on real books —
our fixtures are synthetically generated by a script, so they were never going
to have bookmarks. But it does mean **we have never tested rung 1 against a real
book in CI**. Before we depend on it, we need at least one real textbook with
bookmarks and one without, as permanent fixtures.

### Two things to pin down before trusting it

1. **Pin the library version.** `pypdfium2` is not a direct dependency — it
   arrives indirectly through `pdftext` (`apps/api/pyproject.toml:27`). Version 5
   renamed the exact field we would rely on. A routine upgrade would silently
   break chapter detection. Pin `>=4.30,<5`.
2. **Handle badly-built PDFs.** On some files the fast path silently returns
   nothing where a slower reader recovers the chapters. So: count how many
   chapter starts came back empty, and escalate if the rate is high.

## 4. Why not the alternatives

| Option | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `docling` on the whole document | No | ~29.5 seconds **per page** → over 8 hours for 1,000 pages. Stays restricted to table pages only. |
| `marker` (Datalab) | No | Best structure of the open tools, but the model licence stops being free above ~$5M revenue. A commercial bill with a delayed fuse. |
| `unstructured` | No | Flattens every heading to one level — that distinction is the whole problem. |
| GROBID | No | Trained on academic papers, has no concept of a chapter. Also a separate Java service. |
| LlamaParse | No | **$3–$90 per book** — 1× to 30× our entire per-lesson budget, spent before a single lesson exists. |
| Cloud OCR (AWS/Google/Azure) | No | **$1.50 per 1,000 pages** — half of one lesson's whole $3.00 budget, just to read. |
| Send the whole book to a model | No | ~500,000 words, 10× the cost of rung 5, and does not fit in one request. |
| Silently cut every 30 pages | No | Fine for search, wrong for teaching. Allowed only as a *flagged* last resort. |

### Are we on track?

**The single-student core loop is real and works end to end:** upload → generate
→ play → quiz → teach-back → report. That is genuinely done.

**What does not work is everything that makes it an *adaptive* tutor:** attention,
engagement score, nudges, session close-out, and the report's headline number.
Those are Sprint 3 items and they are further behind than the tracker implies,
because three of them are blocked on each other (25 → 26 → 27) and a fourth (19)
blocks delivery.

Item 30 is a security defect and should be registered and fixed regardless of
the book work.

## 6. Where we break on a 1,000-page book

Five failures, in the order we would hit them.

| # | Failure | The number | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rejected at the door.** We cap uploads at 50 MB. Our test book is 44.7 MB and barely fits; a scanned 1,000-page book is typically 150–400 MB | 50 MB hard limit | `content/router.py:48` |
| 2 | **Table detection blows the budget.** 579 ms per page × 1,000 pages = **9.6 minutes**, and it runs on every page whether or not tables exist | measured | `nodes/extract_subprocess.py` |
| 3 | **A scanned book blows it much harder.** Rendering plus character recognition on 1,000 pages runs to roughly an hour against a 25-minute budget | 2.3× over | `config.py:269-275` |
| 4 | **We would cut a whole book into 15 blobs.** The section limit is 15 for the entire document, whatever its size. If no headings are found, the fallback is **one section titled "Document" containing the whole book**, with no warning | 15 max, 1 on total failure | `config.py:301-310`, `structure_detection.py:119-129` |
| 5 | **A book cannot have chapters until a lesson exists.** The database requires every chapter row to point at a lesson — exactly backwards from what we need | `chapters.lesson_id NOT NULL` | `migrations/20260611000000_initial_schema.sql:132` |

**One more that only appears once we start splitting:** our text extractor cannot
be told "read pages 200 to 260". It takes a file and reads all of it. So if we
split a book into 20 chapter jobs today, **all 20 would re-read the entire book.**
This is the largest single piece of work and it is easy to miss.
(`nodes/extract_subprocess.py:420,511-514`)

**Raising the timeout does not fix any of this.** Our own settings force the
extraction limit to move with the job limit (`config.py:408-417`), so raising the
ceiling just means budgeting two hours for a two-hour job. ADR-002 already says
this and is correct.

## 7. Three things already in our favour

This is cheaper than it looks, because three of the required pieces already exist
and are unused.

1. **The chapters table already has `page_start` and `page_end`.** Someone
   designed this for page-range chapters and then it was never used.
   (`migrations/20260611000000_initial_schema.sql:129-138`)
2. **The extractor already builds per-page text** (`page_texts` in
   `extract_subprocess.py`) and then flattens it into one blob. Slicing by page is
   a matter of *not throwing it away*.
3. **The one database change is in the safe direction.** Making
   `chapters.lesson_id` optional is permissive — it cannot invalidate any existing
   row. No backfill, no downtime.

Estimates in developer-days for one person.

| # | Work item | Days |

| | **Subtotal** | **20 days (~4 weeks for one, ~2 for two)** |

| # | Work item | Days |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | Fallback ladder rungs 2–4 (printed contents page, position/font signals we already discard, chapter scoring) | 6 |

## 9. What it costs to run

### Per book, once

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Chapter-boundary check (only if rungs 1–4 all fail) | $0.0085 |
| Making the text searchable | ~$0.01 |
| Computer time | pennies |
| Character recognition if scanned (our own software, not a paid service) | $0.02–0.05 |
| **Total, once per book** | **under $0.10** |

Paid **once**. If a 20-chapter book serves 20 students on different chapters, the
ingestion cost works out to about **$0.005 per lesson — under one-sixth of one
percent of the $3.00 ceiling.**

### Per lesson

Chapter detection is not a threat to the ceiling; at two cents a book it is a
rounding error. **At the ingestion stage the ceiling is a time-and-memory problem,
not a money problem.** Everything in §6 is measured in minutes and megabytes.

### What we are refusing to pay

| Rejected | Cost | Share of one lesson's $3.00 |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud character recognition | $1.50 / 1,000 pages | **50%, before any teaching happens** |
| LlamaParse | $3–$90 per book | **100%–3,000%** |
| Cloud structured extraction | $10–$70 / 1,000 pages | 3×–23× |

Our own software does the same job for two to five cents. The trade is wall-clock
time, and the fix for wall-clock time is per-chapter jobs — which is exactly what
ADR-002 recommends.

---

## 10. Recommendation

Do the 20-day minimum, in the order listed. Start with item 1, because it is two
days and it tells us immediately whether the whole approach holds on real books.

The one open risk is chapter detection on books with no bookmarks and no clean
text layer. Rungs 2–5 exist for exactly that case and cost under two cents, but
they are unproven on real textbooks. **Spend one day testing rungs 1–4 against
three or four real books before committing to the full plan.**

Everything here gets more expensive the longer we wait, because Dev 2 keeps
building on the assumption that one PDF equals one lesson.

## Appendix — measurement method

All timings on the developer machine, using the project's own virtual environment
(`apps/api/.venv`, pypdfium2 4.30.0).

- Book: *Dive into Deep Learning*, 1,151 pages, 44.7 MB, digitally generated.
- Per-page timings sampled over 25 consecutive pages starting at page 300.
- Chapter-list accuracy checked by opening each of the 27 chapter start pages and
  confirming the title appears in the first 400 characters. 25 matched exactly;
  the 2 that did not were verified by hand to be correct pages where the title
  wraps across a line break. True accuracy 27/27.
- The cheap-model estimate uses 200 characters per page × 1,151 pages = 227,631
  characters ≈ 56,907 tokens at gpt-4o-mini input pricing.

**Not verified:** one of two adversarial verification agents crashed mid-run, so
part of the file-and-line map in §5 comes from a single reading pass. The claims
load-bearing for the decision were re-checked by hand: the absence of any
bookmark reading, the absence of file fingerprinting, the `chapters.lesson_id`
constraint, the 50 MB cap, and every timing above.
